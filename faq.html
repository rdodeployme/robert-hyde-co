Selling

Do you pay cash?

No. Victorian law bans cash payments for scrap metal, and we pay by EFT into your bank account.

Why do you need my ID?

Victoria's scrap metal laws require us to record the seller's name, address and identification, with a description and the weight, for every purchase. A driver licence is easiest, but other ID can be accepted.

How do you work out the price?

By metal type, grade and weight on the day. Prices follow the commodity markets, so we give you a guide price first and the final figure comes from the weighed load.

Is there a minimum amount?

Not for drop-offs. For pickups it depends on the amount and how far away you are. We'll tell you when we call.

What we take

Do you take fridges and air conditioners?

Yes. Fridges and freezers should be clean and empty with the pipes uncut, and the refrigerant is recovered by an ARC-licensed technician before the metal is processed. Air conditioners must be decommissioned and removed by an ARC-licensed technician first.

Do you take car bodies?

Yes, with proof of ownership and clear access for a tilt tray.

What about gas bottles and lithium batteries?

Ask us first. Both are fire risks in a metal load, so they're handled separately or not at all. Never put them in with other scrap.

Do you take asbestos?

No. If we suspect asbestos, for example in an old caravan or shed panels, we won't load it until it has been cleared.

The name

Are you the original Robert Hyde & Co.?

No. The original was a Melbourne marine store dealer active from the 1850s to the 1870s. We're a new business that has taken up the name, and we don't claim its history as our own trading record.

Why revive the name?

Because the trade is the same: buying old metal and sending it back into use. Hyde's left a clear record of that work in Melbourne, and the name had fallen out of use. The time machine on our story page tells it stop by stop.

What was a marine store?

A colonial dealer in second-hand goods and scrap: rope and ships' stores, bottles, rags, bones and metal.
